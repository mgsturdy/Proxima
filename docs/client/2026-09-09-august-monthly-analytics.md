**Site:** proxima.health | **Period:** August 1--31, 2026 (full month) | **Source:** Vercel Web Analytics

> **August broke every record on the books, and this time the growth is human.** July's report called itself the first true baseline month after the June bot wave, and set August up as the first like-for-like month-over-month comparison. That comparison happened, and it is strongly positive: the 24 clean days from August 8 onward ran at **62 pageviews per day, roughly 70% ahead of July's clean window (37/day)**. But the headline is two other days. **On August 4--5 a post on X (Twitter) sent about 1,500 visitors to the homepage in 48 hours**, producing 3,811 pageviews (two-thirds of the month), 142 quiz starts, 40 diagnostic signups, and 10 practitioner inquiries. Unlike June's inflated numbers, every signal says these were real people: 74% on phones, converting through the quiz at the same rate as organic visitors, spread across 88 countries with a normal US majority. August finished at **5,720 pageviews and 2,794 visitors, 4.7x July on both counts.**

- **The spike arrived on Tuesday, August 4 and was gone by Friday.** Referrals from t.co (X's link shortener) went 820 → 634 → 45 → 13 → 11 pv across August 4--8, and 1,525 of the month's 1,560 t.co pageviews landed on the homepage. X's in-app browser was the single most-used "browser" of the month (1,731 pv, 997 visitors). Note that the true X share is larger than the t.co line: once an X visitor clicks to a second page, that pageview records no external referrer, so most of the spike's "direct" traffic is the same audience continuing to browse.
- **August 19 (202 pv, 89 visitors, 31% bounce) was a LinkedIn day.** 26 referrals from linkedin.com and the LinkedIn Android app, 16 from Google, and /about drew 56 pageviews --- people came to see who is behind Proxima. It was the lowest-bounce day of the month by a wide margin. Two visitors also tried /careers and /jobs that day.
- **August 24 (102 pv) and August 28 (111 pv)** were smaller bumps: August 24 was Instagram (6 referrals) plus one visitor from the UAE who read 25 pages; August 28 was the month's best Google day (21 referred pageviews) plus what looks like a practitioner group working through the site (18 pv on /practitioners, 3 inquiries).

## Follow-up on July's recommendations

1. **Fix the quiz feed (homepage / diagnostics CTA experiment).** Not shipped; August's commits were blog posts only, and /diagnostics still carries no quiz link. The August data makes the case louder rather than quieter: the quiz handled 142 starts in two days and converted 28% of a cold audience into diagnostic signups. It is not a capacity or quality problem. It is a routing problem, now four months running.
2. **Decide when to list the blog.** Still unlisted, still zero views, still growing (seven posts in August). Google referrals rose 28% anyway on the core pages, so the domain's search standing is not waiting on the blog. The decision remains open.
3. **Call Long Journey.** Fifth consecutive month, 14 visitors, 13 separate days.
4. **Track the fintech cluster.** Stripe and Ramp both returned; PitchBook joined. Whatever is circulating is still circulating.
5. **Close out the HubSpot purge.** No visibility from the analytics side on whether the June 23--July 8 junk contacts were purged. If they were not, do it before working the August batch: the 37 clean post-mitigation inquiries from July and August are now the working pipeline, and the June 23--July 8 cut is unambiguous.
6. **Stand down on bot work.** Confirmed. The site absorbed 1,842 visitors in two days with no form flood, no crawler geos, and a normal device mix. The German and Austrian crawl signatures have cleared.
7. **August vs. July as the first like-for-like comparison.** Delivered, with an asterisk: the clean-window comparison (Aug 8--31 vs. July 9--31) shows +70% pageviews per day and +63% visitors per day. The full-month comparison is dominated by the spike and should not be used for trend.

## Recommendations for September

1. **Find the August 4 post and do it again.** One X post produced more visitors in 48 hours than the site had seen in any prior month, and it converted. Identify the account and the post, thank or engage the author, and build a repeatable social cadence around the same angle. Add UTM parameters to any owned social links so the next spike is attributable to a specific post rather than to "t.co".
2. **Ship the quiz routing experiment.** Add a quiz CTA to /diagnostics and point the homepage primary CTA at the diagnostics → quiz path. This is the fourth month of recommending it, and August removed the last excuse: the quiz demonstrably converts at scale.
3. **Fix the quiz email screen.** At volume, half of completers skip the email step and a further 12% abandon there. Test a shorter ask, a clearer "what you get" line, or moving the email capture before the results screen. This is the cheapest way to double the contact yield from the same traffic.
4. **Design for mobile first.** Mobile is now 45--66% of pageviews depending on the window, and iOS alone was 53% in August. Walk the homepage → /diagnostics → quiz → email path on an iPhone and fix anything that is not effortless.
5. **Give consumers their own door.** The X audience used the practitioner form because it was the only contact path. A patient-facing "join the waitlist / find a practitioner" entry on /practitioners (or a /contact page, which six visitors went looking for in August) keeps the practitioner pipeline clean and captures consumer demand rather than losing it.
6. **Repeat the LinkedIn post monthly.** August 19 brought 89 visitors at 31% bounce, the most engaged day of the month, and it is fully under Proxima's control.
7. **Check the practitioner-onboarding pipeline.** /physicianagreement went from 10 views to zero and JotForm referrals fell 12 → 5, while inquiries tripled. If the July cohort stalled in the paperwork, it will show up on your side before it shows up here.
8. **Keep the search engine running.** Google referrals are compounding at 20--30% a month regardless of everything else. The blog listing decision, and a Google Search Console pull to see which queries are landing on the core pages, are the two levers.
